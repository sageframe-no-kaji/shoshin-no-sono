# ho-07.2 — World-then-writing emergence

The map remembers how it was made. ho-07 rendered the static, fully-populated
territory; this ho animates its becoming. On load, the terrain emerges in the order
the work was conceived: peaks rise from the floor one beat at a time in `conceived`
order, renamed works pulse a second time when they were `named`, and once the world
has finished rising the six writing towns arrive over it in their own dated order.
The data this reads — `conceived` on every work, `named` on the two renamed ones —
landed in ho-07.1. This ho adds the rendering; the data layer is already in place.

The rhythm is **even beats**. The animation reads the dates only to *sort* and to
detect *simultaneity* — it never spaces by absolute time. A three-year gap and a
single day get the same beat, so the 2022 outlier and the fuzzy early months never
distort the pacing. Same-day conceptions share one beat (ho-system + kanyo,
2025-12-12; shodō + sutra, 2026-03-04).

**Out of scope:**
- **Data, schema, or Indexer changes.** ho-07.1 finished the data. A read-only
  Indexer accessor is allowed only if a query genuinely needs one; no new fields, no
  re-dating, no edges.
- **Relationship features as terrain (ho-08)** — ridges, roads, trails, twin peaks.
  The emergence renders peaks and towns rising; the connections between them are ho-08.
- **Click interaction and cards (ho-09).**
- **The `created` sweep** (later era) — the animation reads `conceived`, not the
  unreliable `created` estimates, so it is not blocked on that sweep.
- **Mobile cartography.** Per the System Design, full cartography is desktop-only; the
  narrow-viewport path keeps its simplified static render (no emergence).

**Resolves deferred decisions** (from the ho-overview's ho-07 entry, as reframed by
the ho-07.1 update note):
- The **chronological populate animation**, reframed from towns-only to
  world-then-writing — peaks rise on `conceived`, towns arrive after.
- The **rename pulse** — how a work conceived under one name and `named` later reads
  on the map (Decision 2, Decision 5).
- The **pre-history floor** — how the 2022 outlier and the 2025-11-11 corpus-floor
  landmark sit relative to the dense emergence (Decision 5).

## Phase 1 — Think

### Decision 1 — Module layout: a pure `emergence.js` timeline; a player in the page; the Cartographer and Indexer untouched.

One new module, parallel to ho-07's `settlements.js`/`settlement-map.js` split between
pure data and render:

- **`src/emergence.js`** — pure, no DOM, no timers. Queries the Indexer for
  `conceived`/`named` and produces an ordered **beat timeline**: a list of beats, each
  beat a set of *events* (`rise` of one or more peaks, `pulse` of a renamed peak, or
  `arrive` of one or more towns). Same-day events collapse into one beat; the world
  beats come first (peak rises and pulses, merge-sorted by date), then the writing
  beats (town arrivals by `conceived` = `publication_date`). The 2025-11-11 corpus
  floor is injected here as a constant, not read from data (Decision 5). Fully testable
  from a fixture Indexer — feed it works with known `conceived`/`named` and assert the
  beat order, the same-day collapse, and the world→writing partition.

- **The player** lives in `src/cartography-page.js`, next to the existing `render()`.
  It is the only stateful, timer-driven piece: it walks the timeline beat by beat,
  drives a `requestAnimationFrame` loop for the continuous rise (Decision 3), and calls
  the existing render path each frame with a per-frame reveal state. It owns
  cancellation and the reduced-motion branch (Decision 6).

The **Cartographer stays pure** — it already computes the field and towns
deterministically; the only change is an additive, optional hook so the player can
scale amplitudes per frame (Decision 3). The **Indexer is untouched**: it already
returns `conceived`/`named` via `getWork()`, and emergence/animation are render
concerns that stay out of it (CLAUDE.md boundary — the Indexer does not encode
cartographic or rendering semantics). The **Gate is untouched**: emergence triggers on
load and reseed, not on URL state (Decision 6).

### Decision 2 — Beat schedule: merge-sort rise (`conceived`) and pulse (`named`) into the world sequence; towns follow by date; even beats throughout.

The timeline is built in two partitions:

- **World** — every peak work (non-`writing` group) contributes a `rise` event keyed by
  `conceived`. Every work with a `named` date contributes a `pulse` event keyed by
  `named`. Both kinds merge-sort into one date-ordered sequence, then collapse to beats
  by equal date. So forteller and pālana **rise** together at their ~2025-08-01 beat and
  **pulse** together later, at the ordinal slot where 2026-03-21 falls among the other
  `conceived` dates (between reading-instrument's rise and glassroom's). The pulse is a
  second beat for an already-risen peak, not a new peak.
- **Writing** — the six `writing`-group towns contribute `arrive` events keyed by
  `conceived` (which equals `publication_date` for writing). the-empty-container and
  judgment-at-scale share 2026-04-15 → one beat.

Beats play on **even intervals** — the beat *index* drives timing, never the date. The
date is used only to order and to group. This is the same even-beat semantics the
chronology fixed: pacing is ordinal, legibility is constant, and provisional/fuzzy
dates cost nothing.

A beat is `{ index, kind: 'rise' | 'pulse' | 'arrive', ids: string[], date: string }`.
The world partition is all rise/pulse beats in order; the writing partition is all
arrive beats; the player plays world, then a held beat, then writing (Decision 4).

### Decision 3 — Rise model: continuous. Amplitude eases 0→full and the heightfield re-contours during each rising beat. Perf-gated in execution, discrete fallback named.

A peak rises by having its field amplitude (`importance × filter_relevance`) ease from
0 to full over its beat, with the terrain re-derived as it grows — the land lifting out
of the floor, contours blooming outward. The clean hook: extend `computeField` in
`cartographer.js` with an **optional, additive** `opts.emergenceScale?: (id: string) =>
number` (default `() => 1`), folded into the amplitude as
`importance × relevance × emergenceScale(id)`. Existing callers are unaffected; the
player passes a scale map that the rAF loop updates each frame. A not-yet-risen peak has
scale 0 and is simply absent from the heightfield. A peak's label (and debug dot)
appears as its rise completes.

The continuous path means re-running `buildHeightfield` + contour extraction
(`contours.js` → `contour-map.js`) repeatedly. **This is the one real performance risk
in the ho** (see Discovery). The decision is to *target* continuous rise and gate it on
a real perf check at execution; if the desktop target can't sustain smooth re-contouring
for the corpus, fall back to **discrete** — one recompute per beat, the peak appearing
at full on its beat — which is cheap (~one recompute per world beat) and still reads as
punctuated emergence. The fallback is a degradation of the same timeline and player, not
a redesign.

### Decision 4 — Handoff: world fully rises, a held beat, then the towns arrive over it.

Literal world-then-writing. All peak rises and pulses play to completion; the risen
terrain holds for one beat; then the writing towns fade in over the finished terrain in
date order. Town arrival reuses ho-07's settlement render (`settlement-map.js`) revealed
progressively — the player passes a revealed-town set with a per-town fade alpha; towns
not yet arrived are not drawn. This is the towns-fade-in gesture the ho-07 entry named
and deferred here.

### Decision 5 — Pre-history floor: aspirational-intelligence and the 2025-11-11 corpus floor are present from the first frame; the dense emergence rises over them.

The deep past is a quiet floor, not an opening beat. Before beat 1:

- **aspirational-intelligence** (2022-11-04, archived, importance 2) is already on the
  terrain at full — a faint, low feature on the floor, the work that predates the
  practice. It does not rise; it is the ground the emergence happens above.
- **The corpus floor** — first Claude chat, 2025-11-11 — renders as a faint horizon
  marker/label, an **animation constant injected in `emergence.js`, not a `works.json`
  entry**. It marks where the dense record begins.

The sparse early beats rise over this floor: sageframe (~2025-04), then forteller/pālana
(~2025-08). The dense run begins at kanyo + ho-system (2025-12-12). The exact visual
weight of the floor and the marker is by-feel (Decision 7) — the architectural call is
that pre-history is ambient ground present from frame 0, not the first event in the
sequence.

### Decision 6 — Lifecycle: emergence plays on load and reseed only; filter changes render statically; cancellation and reduced-motion snap to the final state.

Emergence is an intro gesture. It runs once on initial load and again on reseed (a fresh
layout earns a fresh becoming). **Filter changes do not re-animate** — they render
statically through the existing path (the ho-06/ho-07 recede behavior), because filtering
is exploration, not re-narration, and re-emerging on every toggle tires. If a filter
change or reseed lands mid-emergence, the player **cancels cleanly and snaps to the
final fully-risen state** before applying the new render. `prefers-reduced-motion`
short-circuits the whole animation to the final static state — same end frame, no motion.

### Decision 7 — Timing, easing, amplitudes, floor weight: by-feel tuners, seeded with defaults, landed in execution.

Following the ho-07 tuner posture: beat duration, inter-beat gap, rise easing curve,
pulse flash shape and duration, the world→writing held-beat length, the pre-history
floor opacity, and the corpus-floor marker weight are all dialed by feel against the
visual register, not committed silently. Wire them into the existing tuner panel in
`cartography-page.js` (the same panel ho-07 used to land label scale and recession).
Seed sane starting values (a beat in the low-hundreds-of-ms, a total world run in the
several-seconds range) so the first frame is watchable, then tune live.

### Discovery (deferred to execution) — the continuous-rise performance budget.

Decision 3 targets continuous re-contouring; whether that holds is a measurement, not a
guess. At execution, profile the real cost: how many `buildHeightfield` + contour
extractions per second the desktop target sustains for the full peak set, and whether a
rising beat can re-contour at an acceptable frame rate. Inspect, then choose the recompute
cadence — per-frame, throttled to a fixed step, or (if it fails) the discrete fallback.
This gate is the first real thing the execution session resolves, because it determines
the shape of the player loop.

## Phase 2 — Execute

One building session, framed by this document — **no agent tasks**. This is feel-heavy
and perf-sensitive (continuous rise, live tuners, the perf gate); it wants one inhabited
session the practitioner watches, not farmed-out specs. The natural first slice is the
pure `emergence.js` timeline (build it test-first against a fixture Indexer); the player,
the continuous-rise integration, the tuners, and accessibility follow in the same session.

The work, in order:

1. **`src/emergence.js`** — the pure timeline builder (Decisions 1, 2, 5). Test-first:
   beat order, same-day collapse, world→writing partition, rise/pulse for forteller +
   pālana, the injected corpus-floor constant, the pre-history floor set.
2. **`computeField` hook** — the additive optional `emergenceScale` in `cartographer.js`
   (Decision 3), with a test that scale 0 removes a peak from the field and scale 1
   reproduces the existing field exactly (no regression for current callers).
3. **The perf gate** — measure continuous re-contour cost (Discovery); choose the
   cadence; if it fails, switch the player to the discrete path. Log the measurement and
   the choice as a surfacing.
4. **The player** in `cartography-page.js` — walk the timeline, drive the rAF loop with
   the per-frame scale map and the revealed-town set, render through the existing path,
   handle the world→writing handoff (Decision 4), cancellation, and reduced-motion
   (Decision 6).
5. **Tuners** — wire the timing/easing/floor parameters into the existing tuner panel
   (Decision 7); land the values by feel against the register.

### Testing and iteration approach

`emergence.js` and the `computeField` hook are pure — unit-tested to the 90% floor like
the rest of `src/`, from fixtures, no DOM. The player is timer- and DOM-driven; test its
*logic* (next-beat selection, cancellation snapping to final state, the reduced-motion
short-circuit producing the same end frame as a completed run) with injectable
time/`requestAnimationFrame` the way `createCartographer` already injects `randomSeed`.
The *feel* (easing, pacing, floor weight) is dialed live in the browser against the
register, not asserted in tests. Verification rhythm each step: lint, typecheck, test
(≥90%), then commit, prefixed `ho-07.2:`.

### Done means

- On load and on reseed, the world rises in `conceived` order on even beats; same-day
  conceptions (ho-system + kanyo, shodō + sutra) share a beat.
- forteller and pālana rise at their ~2025-08 beat and pulse at the 2026-03-21 ordinal
  slot.
- The world finishes, holds, then the six writing towns arrive in date order over the
  terrain.
- aspirational-intelligence and the 2025-11-11 corpus-floor marker are present from the
  first frame; the dense run rises over them.
- Filter changes render statically; a filter or reseed mid-emergence snaps to the final
  state; `prefers-reduced-motion` shows the final state with no motion.
- The continuous-rise perf result is measured and the cadence (or discrete fallback)
  recorded as a surfacing.
- `npm run lint`, `npm run typecheck`, `npm test` (≥90%) all green; commits atomic and
  `ho-07.2:`-prefixed.
