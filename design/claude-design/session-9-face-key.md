You are doing bounded visual design exploration for a procedurally generated
topographic map. One question only in this session:

WHAT DOES THE FACE KEY LOOK LIKE?

(Session 9. Runs AFTER session 8 — the cartouche — because the two are
siblings: the key is composed knowing the cartouche's chosen variant, may
share its corner or take another margin, and must never outweigh it. The key
is the map face's MINIMAL apparatus — quad-sheet style, a few lines near the
margin. The FULL legend lives on the back of the map (the verso) and is not
this session's question.)

Context (all you need): The map renders a body of creative work as pen-and-ink
cartography on cream paper — hand-surveyed atlas register. Streamline hachures
carry relief; faint iso-lines structure elevation; the landmass is bounded by
a sea with a fine ink coastline and engraved wave-combs (session 7). The map's
marks are semantic: PEAKS are works (height = importance); TOWNS are the
writing, settled at the foot of what they document; TRAILS (a fine rail with
perpendicular rungs) climb from a town to the peak it documents, and run
between peaks that validate one another; ROADS (a cased double-line) join
companion writings; a small amber BEACON burns at each summit. The key sits on
the face, in open water near the margin, on a cream reserve that parts the
wave texture (the clearing-as-paint-over mechanism the whole register uses).
A visitor who reads the key should be able to read the map; a visitor who
ignores it should barely notice it.

Real content (draft wording — the session may tighten the words, not the
meanings; fewer entries is better than more):
- Peaks are works; height is editorial weight.
- Towns are the writing, settled beneath what they document.
- Trails climb to what a writing documents; peaks that validate one another
  are joined by trail.
- Roads join companion writings.
- (optional fifth) The beacon marks a summit.

Hard constraints:
- Output is inline SVG only. Self-contained, no external font files — system
  font-stacks only (serif character welcome; flag any webfont desire as one
  line, don't embed). Each variant in a 320×200 viewBox.
- Palette: cream ground #FDFCF9, ink in warm grays (#6B6B6B to #2B2B2B);
  water ink #8A7B6A may appear ONLY inside a wave-context swatch; terracotta
  #9A5B3C only if a swatch genuinely requires it (the city landmark) — never
  as key decoration. Beacon amber #D4952A only inside the beacon swatch.
- NO ornament. Ornament is licensed to the cartouche alone; the key is
  working apparatus — rules, type, and swatches, nothing else.
- NO scale bar and NO north arrow. On this map horizontal distance and
  orientation encode nothing (positions are seeded scatter; the features
  carry the meaning) — a scale or compass would be a fake-semantic mark, the
  class this register outlaws. Do not invent them.
- Swatches are SAMPLES of the map's actual marks, drawn at the map's own
  character and weight: a trail fragment = thin rail + 3–4 perpendicular
  rungs (~0.6px weight); a road fragment = cased double-line (~4.5px rails on
  a cream casing); a town = 4–6 small ink blocks, party-wall gaps; a peak =
  a few short hachure strokes radiating, or 2 concentric contour arcs; the
  beacon = a soft amber dot. In implementation these will be GENERATED by the
  same renderers that draw the map, so the key can never drift — design
  toward that truth.
- The key must be procedurally placeable: a self-contained SVG group with a
  fixed aspect ratio, dropped near a margin at variable scale, subordinate to
  the cartouche at every scale.
- Typography follows the map's label register (Spectral-class serif): entry
  labels small, tracked, unhurried. The key whispers.

Explore and deliver:
- 3 variants, labeled A–C, each with a one-line caption naming the choice it
  makes. Vary exactly these three axes:
  1. FRAMING — unframed floating list vs a single hairline rule above/below
     vs a thin rule box (no double rules — that is cartouche language).
  2. ENTRY COUNT AND VOICE — 3 entries vs 5; label wording tight vs fuller.
  3. SWATCH TREATMENT — mark-sample beside each label vs prose-only vs
     sample-plus-label sharing a baseline.
- Then your recommendation: which variant reads as the working key of a
  serious survey — apparatus, not ornament — and why, in 3 sentences max.

Judging criteria (design toward these):
1. It reads as apparatus: the visitor consults it, then forgets it.
2. Subordinate to the cartouche at every plausible pairing and scale — half
   size still legible, never competing.
3. The swatches read as genuine samples of the surrounding map, not icons.
4. Sitting on open water, its cream reserve holds a deliberate edge against
   the wave texture.
5. Every entry earns its line; three good lines beat five adequate ones.

Out of scope — do not touch: JSON, data models, architecture, animation,
interactivity, the cartouche itself (assume it exists nearby; compose against
its presence, don't redesign it), the verso's full legend, the sea, peaks,
settlements beyond swatch samples. If you have ideas about those, put them in
one line at the end under "parked" and stop.
