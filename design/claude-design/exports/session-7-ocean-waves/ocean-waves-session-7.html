Procedural cartography · ocean fill

Engraved waves for the sea, drawn by rule and by chance.

After the old copperplate manner: bold sinuous crest lines run parallel to the shore — each band an offset copy of the coast, relaxing toward an open swell as it marches out to sea — and off each swell a comb of fine hair‑strokes rides the coast normal, so it always meets the crest square-on. A rounded headland bends the shoreline mid-frame; watch the waves and their combs swing to follow its normal. Every stroke carries a little jitter of position, length and curve, so no two are alike. Six characters of sea below; each has a tuner, and Reseed rolls fresh randomness while keeping the character.

One coastline in every frame; the ocean side is filled by the generator. Bands are offset copies of the shore, each blended a step further toward a straight swell, so the sea calms as it opens out. Every feather points from its crest to the next band along the shared normal and is auto‑capped by that gap — which is why the comb thickens where bands spread (the headland tip) and thins where they pinch, exactly as the burin laid it. Palette is ink on warm rag; no colour but the sea. Drop any option's SVG into your map as the water layer. Parked: closed bays/lakes, wave interaction with islands, and concave inlets where offset bands would need to be trimmed for self‑crossing.
