Session 9 · Face Key

What does the face key look like?

The map face’s minimal apparatus — quad-sheet style, a few lines near the margin. It sits on the sea from Session 7 on a cream reserve that parts the wave texture (the register’s clearing-as-paint-over), and it runs after the cartouche because the two are siblings: the key must never outweigh the title block. The full legend lives on the verso; the face only names enough to read the sheet. The swatches are samples of the map’s own marks — hachure, ink blocks, the rail, the cased road — drawn at the map’s character and weight, so the key can never drift from the territory. Three variants vary exactly three axes: framing, entry count & voice, and swatch treatment. No ornament, no scale bar, no north arrow — on this map distance and orientation encode nothing.

Recommendation
A Unframed reserve · three primitives · mark-samples in the gutter

A is the working key of a serious survey. The cream clearing is its only edge, exactly how a quad-sheet key sits on open water — so it never puts a frame beside the cartouche’s, and stays plainly subordinate at any pairing. Three lines name the three primitives a reader actually needs — the work, the writing, the documentation link — and let the verso carry road and beacon, honouring “three good lines beat five.” And because each swatch is the map’s own hachure, ink blocks and rail at map weight, they read as samples lifted from the sheet, not icons.

Placement check · variant A beside the cartouche

The working case: cartouche and key share the same open water at opposite margins. The key must hold its cream edge against the wave texture and read as apparatus a visitor consults and forgets — visibly the junior of the two reserves, never competing with the title block for the corner of the eye.
