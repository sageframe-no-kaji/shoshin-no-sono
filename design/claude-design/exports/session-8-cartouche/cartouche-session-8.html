Session 8 · Cartouche

What does the cartouche look like?

The map’s title block — the one place ornament is allowed. It sits in an open-water corner, so each variant is shown on the sea: the engraved wave texture from Session 7 runs up to a clean cream reserve and stops, the waves parting around the block rather than running beneath it (the register’s clearing-as-paint-over). The native title 初心の園 leads at every size; Spectral carries the roman, from Session 3. Three variants vary exactly three axes — frame treatment, four-line hierarchy, and accent & register reference. Each cartouche is one self-contained SVG group with a fixed 8:5 aspect the pipeline drops into a corner at any scale.

Recommendation
A Open reserve · terracotta rule · brush chop & hachure

A signs with the quietest authority. An unframed clearing is exactly how a real chart cartouche sits on open water — the reserve is the frame — and a single terracotta rule is the one permitted flourish, so it signs the territory without becoming a poster the way the double-ruled plate (B) begins to. The brush chop signs the territory and the hachure fan says “this is the kind of map this is” in a whisper, and at half size in a corner the block still steps cleanly from native to tagline without stealing the sea.

Placement check · variant A

The recommended cartouche dropped into the corner of a wider open-water plate at roughly half scale — the working case. It should hold its edge against the wave texture and read as a deliberate reserve, not a hole in the sea, without pulling the eye off the territory.
