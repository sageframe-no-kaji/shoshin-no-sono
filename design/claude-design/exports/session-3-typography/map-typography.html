Session 3 · Typography

What does the map’s lettering look like?

One face only — Spectral, the register’s established serif, with Georgia as the honest fallback; the native gloss rides a CJK mincho stack chosen to sit with it. No new typeface is introduced. Each panel applies the same three label classes — peak (latin + native gloss), settlement (latin, smaller), and elevation figure — to the real content, varying only the serif character, the latin/native pairing, and how the hierarchy is built. Everything is procedural: one rule per class, applied by a pipeline.

Recommendation
B Engraved spaced caps · native beside

B reads most like an old survey plate. Wide-tracked roman caps are the engraver’s long-standing convention for a named summit, and setting the native gloss beside the latin at near-equal optical size keeps it a co-equal name rather than a footnote. It also carries the whole hierarchy without boxes or colour — peaks own the cap-height and tracking, settlements step down to a tighter, smaller cap, and the elevation stays a quiet italic figure — and the settlement caps still hold at 60%, as the check below confirms.
