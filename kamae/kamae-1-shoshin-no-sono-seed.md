## The Friction

The work is interrelated in a way that is the brand and the identity. The relationships between the pieces are not decoration. They are the argument the body of work makes about itself — that what looks like fifteen projects is one project, prosecuted across software, methodology, writing, and websites. Right now nobody can see that. To see it requires reading everything everywhere — the essays at sageframe.substack.com, the manifesto at pinkteaming.net, the research home at atmarcus.net, the repos at github.com/sageframe-no-kaji, the live demos at the subdomains — and nobody is going to do that.

The current sageframe.net is an 80s MIT personal page. Honest, but doing none of the jobs the body of work actually needs. The dispersal of the work across surfaces is correct — each piece belongs at the surface that fits it. But the dispersal makes the body of work illegible *as a body*. The illegibility is the problem.

Shoshin no Sono exists to make the coherence legible without recentralizing anything. To route, not to host. To demonstrate that the work is one project by rendering the relationships as cartography, and to send visitors through that cartography to the canonical homes where the work itself lives.

The brand is the relationships. The relationships are invisible. Shoshin no Sono fixes that.

## The Landscape

A scan of what exists in this territory, with honest attribution.

**Bret Victor — worrydream.com.** Curated, hand-built, philosophically through-lined. Relationships between projects are made through prose, not data. No graph view. Strong precedent for "personal work site as serious intellectual artifact." Different mechanism from what Shoshin no Sono is doing, but the seriousness register is shared.

**Andy Matuschak — notes.andymatuschak.org.** Bidirectional linking, graph view as a navigable structure for intellectual work. Strong precedent for *thinking as graph*; weaker as a projects portfolio because his works are notes, not shipped systems. The graph is a secondary navigation, not the front door.

**Maggie Appleton — maggieappleton.com.** Digital garden with explicit relationships between Notes, Essays, Patterns. The graph view exists but is secondary; categorical navigation is primary. *Note: Shoshin no Sono predates encounter with Maggie Appleton's Digital Garden. The naming and concept descend from Book 5 (Shoshin: The Garden of Knowledge and Creation) of the Sageframe knowledge system, written before encountering her work. The shared noun "garden" is older than either of us — cultivated knowledge has been a garden metaphor for at least a millennium.*

**Glenn McDonald — *Every Noise at Once* (defunct).** Catalog of music genres rendered as a typed-adjacency map with click-through to canonical Spotify pages. Different domain; same structural pattern: catalog-as-map, click-to-leave architecture, taxonomy-as-argument. Closest functional analogue Shoshin no Sono has.

**Connected Papers / ResearchRabbit.** Typed-edge graph navigation of academic literature — enter at one node, see the neighborhood, navigate outward via typed edges. Closest structural analogue. But built for academic paper graphs, not for a personal body of work with the practitioner's presence as part of the substance.

**Italo Calvino — *Invisible Cities*.** Not a website but a structural precedent. One Venice refracted through fifty-five descriptions, each internally complete, none of them *the* city, together they are. Marco Polo describes the same place to Kublai Khan from different angles; the reader is Khan, trying to grasp the whole. This maps directly onto what Shoshin no Sono's filter mechanic does — one body of work, refracted through different lenses (themes, media, time), each lens producing a complete and different view.

**Cartographic precedents for the visual register.** Saul Steinberg's *Voyage* drawings. Joost Grootens's atlases. Olaus Magnus's Carta Marina (1539). Vintage surveyor's pen-work. Specifically, a serious technical cartography register — contour lines and elevation, towns rendered as small urban masses, restrained typography, no fantasy decoration. Not Tolkien. Not Earthsea. Not the Province of Caeldor. Cartography as a real craft of representation, applied to a real body of work.

**The gap, named honestly.** Each element above has precedent. The combination — typed relationships *and* network-first navigation *and* click-out-to-canonical-homes *and* visible architectural argument *and* procedurally generated visualization that responds to filter as a fresh survey of the same territory — is not unprecedented in any single element, but the combination, for this body of work, is. The honest claim is not "this is novel." The honest claim is: this combination, for this body of work, makes the coherence legible in a way the dispersed sources can't.

## The Vision

Shoshin no Sono is the public-facing demonstration that the body of work is one project — an epistemology of learning, creation, and craft, manifest across software, methodology, writing, and websites.

The site is a communication tool. Not a destination. Not a research blog. Not a portfolio. Not a brand front. Not a thinking-aid for the maker. It is the surface on which the coherence of the work becomes legible to someone who has not yet read everything everywhere. The visitor encounters the territory, reads the relationships, clicks through to the work itself. The catalog has done its job when the visitor has gone somewhere else.

The visual centerpiece is **interference cartography** — procedurally generated topographic maps where each filter produces a fresh survey of the same body of work. Works are peaks, with elevation derived from incoming relationship density and filter relevance. Writing pieces are towns, populating chronologically by publication date as the map renders. Typed relationships become cartographic features: ridges for lineage, trails for validation, twin peaks for paired works, roads between towns for writing-to-writing reference. Each render is fresh; same data, different drawing; all honest, none canonical. The map remakes itself on each encounter.

The site replaces the current sageframe.net prototype.

## Identity

The project is **Shoshin no Sono** — 初心の園 — **the Garden of Beginner's Mind.**

The morphology:

- **初心 (shoshin)** — beginner's mind; openness, humility, receptive awareness. The Zen term for the state a student brings to learning, a mind unburdened by assumption. Shunryū Suzuki: *"In the beginner's mind there are many possibilities, in the expert's mind there are few."*
- **の (no)** — possessive and relational particle; "of."
- **園 (sono / en)** — garden, grove, cultivated enclosure. Not a wild place. Tended, but alive.

The site is the garden — *sono* — where Shoshin manifests as legible. The practice itself, the body of work, *is* Shoshin. The site is the demonstration of the practice. Shoshin Garden in English when the kanji would be opaque; **Shoshin no Sono** when the full name belongs in the room.

The native-script naming sits inside the convention the body of work already uses: Kanyō 観鷹, Hōzō 宝蔵, Dandori 段取り, Shodō 書道, Kiku 聴く, Ho 歩, Kinhin 経行, Sutra 経. Shoshin no Sono completes the set. The names are not decoration. Each names a discipline of attention — contemplating, treasuring, ordering, brushing, listening, walking, beginning, threading. The site is the one of these that demonstrates the others.

**Brand architecture clarification.** This conversation arrived at a cleaner allocation than the overloaded one the project started with. Four properties, four jobs:

- **Sageframe** is the studio. The research-and-development "company." The umbrella under which the work gets made.
- **Shoshin** is what the studio makes. The projects, the body of work, the inquiry.
- **Blueprint for Creativity** is the consulting practice.
- **atmarcus.net** is the professional landing site.

This is a tighter brand story than the project started with. Sageframe is no longer doing six jobs. It is doing one. Shoshin no Sono, by making the body of work legible, lets each property do its own job clearly.

## Project Nature and Intent

Open source. Personal, in the sense that one person is building it; public, in the sense that everything it surfaces is public work.

**This is a way of thinking, not just a website.** The repo lives in `sageframe-no-kaji` because Shoshin no Sono is software, methodology, and a thinking-tool — not just a deployable site. The static output of the tool deploys to `sageframe-dharma`. The separation matters: the tool is the work; the deployed catalog is one of its products. Shoshin no Sono cataloging Shoshin software is itself a Shoshin object — the methodology demonstrated on its own contents.

The recursive payoff is real. Shoshin no Sono is built using the Ho System practice (small bounded sessions, agent partnership inside architectural authority). Each addition of a new work to the catalog is itself a ~30-minute ho. The maintenance of the catalog is part of the body of work the catalog catalogs.

## Architecture Direction

The technical and architectural decisions arrived at through the seed conversation. Provisional where marked.

### Source and deployment

- **Source repo:** `sageframe-no-kaji/shoshin-no-sono` (name provisional; finalize in System Design)
- **Deployment target:** `sageframe-dharma/sageframe.net`
- **Hosting:** Cloudflare Pages, preview deploys on the `labs` branch, production on `main`
- **Branch discipline:** `v0-legacy` tag preserves current prototype state; `labs` branch holds rebuild; merge to `main` retires prototype and deploys Shoshin no Sono as production

### Data

- One JSON file (`works.json`) holds all data. Schema drafted at `sageframe_labs_schema.json` (v3).
- Unified `works` collection with a `media` array declaring form (software · writing · website · image · talk · methodology). Hybrid works are honest about being hybrid.
- **Relationships are typed directional edges, stored once on the source work.** Some types are symmetric (`paired_with`, `companion_to`); the renderer treats them as undirected at render time. Some are directional (`descends_from`, `succeeded_by`, `validates`, `documents`, `operationalizes`). Optional per-edge `strength` overrides the type default.
- **Each project's entry is not fixed once authored.** Edges get added to existing entries when relationships emerge later. But adding a new work does not require editing every existing entry — only the new entry plus a small, LLM-identified set of existing entries whose stories extend through the new work.
- **Themes** (controlled vocabulary, 1–3 per work) drive filter-based clustering. Distinct from tags.
- **Tags** are open vocabulary, categorical.
- **Work groups** are exclusive categorical bins, one per work, used for section placement in grid view.
- The N×N relationship space the practitioner imagines (every work weighted against every other) is a *derived view*, computed at render time from the edge list plus theme/tag adjacency. It is not stored. It is the math the visualization runs on, regenerated fresh each time.

### Visualization — interference cartography

The primary view is procedurally generated cartographic topography in the pen-and-ink register.

- **Each work is a peak.** Elevation computed from a heightfield that sums radial functions (Gaussian or wave-interference) centered at each work, with amplitudes set by importance × filter relevance. Marching-squares extracts contour lines from the heightfield. Same mathematical family as the Resonance Field on atmarcus.net; the two visualizations are aesthetic siblings, not data siblings.
- **Each render is a fresh survey.** Filter to "craft" → one Venice. Filter again → a different Venice, both honest to the same data. The Calvino discipline. The map remakes itself. Screenshots are valid drawings but not canonical artifacts.
- **Writing pieces are towns.** They populate after the terrain renders, in publication date order. The visitor watches the writing arrive in the order it was written, across the territory. The map carries the history of the thinking-out-loud, not just the topology of the work.
- **Relationship types become cartographic features.**
  - `descends_from` → ridge running from parent peak to child peak (lineage as walkable terrain)
  - `validates` → trail from valley to summit (the journey to the proof)
  - `paired_with` → twin peaks at similar elevation, shared col
  - `companion_to` → adjacent towns sharing a road
  - `operationalizes` → town at the foot of the peak it operationalizes
  - `documents` → road from town back to the peak it describes
  - `succeeded_by` → ridge with directional notation
- **Adding a new work restarts the topography.** A new peak enters the range; the territory updates and continues updating. The body of work is alive in time, not just in encounter.
- **Visitor-as-Marco-Polo (deferred).** The physics supports interactive peak-dragging — visitor briefly drawing the city their own way. Whether to expose this interaction is a UX decision for System Design, not the seed. Available; not committed.

### Aesthetic register

The visual touchstone is a serious surveyor's draft. Pen-and-ink contour hatching, restrained typography, no fantasy decoration. Specifically the Stonecrow Hill family of reference: cream paper, warm-gray contour lines, terracotta for the inhabited features (towns), DM Serif Display for the title cartouche only, IBM Plex Sans for body copy, IBM Plex Mono for elevation labels and metadata. Aligned with the existing brand system from atmarcus.net and pinkteaming.net.

### Alternative views

- **Grid view** — cards organized by `work_group`. Honest list navigation. Available via header toggle. Default on mobile.
- **About page** — the four-property brand architecture explained; the project's identity and intent stated plainly; the Maggie Appleton attribution note included.
- Other views may emerge in System Design (a chronological view, a lineage view). Not committed in the seed.

### CMS and authoring

Mandatory, not optional. The phasing changes from the original handoff: CMS sits at Phase 2, before ship-ready polish.

- **CMS:** Sveltia or Decap at `/admin/`, GitHub OAuth, commits trigger Cloudflare rebuild. Choice deferred to System Design.
- **AI-assist:** A Claude API endpoint that receives a partial new entry plus the full current `works.json` plus a clear prompt template. The model proposes the new entry's outgoing relationships, themes, tags, work group, importance estimate; *and* it proposes reverse-direction edge additions to existing entries when an existing project's story should extend through the new work. The author reviews a structured diff. Accept, reject, edit. Commit.
- **Run discipline:** Whether the AI-assist runs as a local script (Python or Node) or as a GitHub Action endpoint reachable from the CMS — deferred to System Design.
- **The workflow is itself a Ho System practice.** Adding a new work to Shoshin no Sono is a bounded ~30-minute session: brief the agent (partial entry), agent proposes implementation (relationships, themes, work group placement), practitioner holds architectural authority (review and accept/reject), commit, devlog. The catalog's maintenance discipline is the methodology it demonstrates.

### Tech stack

- Vanilla HTML/JS/CSS. No build step required for the deployed site.
- `d3-force` for the underlying graph physics; rendered as topography via SVG marching-squares and procedural contour generation. Loaded via ESM CDN.
- One JSON data file. Schema validation at edit time (CMS-side and editor-side).
- No backend services beyond the CMS admin route. No database. No auth except CMS-side.
- Static site only. Progressive enhancement — grid view works without JS; cartographic view requires JS.

## Constraints

What reality imposes.

- Solo build. No budget for outside design or development help.
- No date pressure. The bar is "when it carries the work."
- Static site, no backend, no databases, no auth except CMS-side.
- All data in repo, version-controlled. No cloud-only state.
- Performance: initial payload under 500KB including d3-force module and `works.json`. Time-to-interactive under 2 seconds on a good connection. The site should feel instant once loaded.
- Must not duplicate content from canonical homes (always-link architecture). The catalog routes; it does not republish.
- Practitioner skill stack: strong systems thinking, vanilla web (atmarcus.net and pinkteaming.net already built and deployed), Python, Swift. **New territory:** d3-force as a library; cartographic procedural generation (heightfield → contour extraction); Sveltia/Decap CMS with GitHub OAuth; Claude API call integration (programmatic, not Claude Code).

The new-territory list is real. None of it is foreign — adjacent to skills already in hand — but none is currently in muscle memory.

## Success Criteria

How the project will be known to have worked.

1. **The site is up at sageframe.net** and carries all current writing, all current repos, all current websites, plus the work that has been built since the current prototype was last updated. Completeness, not novelty, is the floor.
2. **A stranger arriving cold can leave the catalog understanding the body of work as one project.** The coherence claim has been transmitted. The visitor would describe the work, if asked, as something other than "a portfolio."
3. **The site does not feel dated.** The visualization reads as cartographic and architectural, not as a 2014 D3 graph demo or as a Tolkien fantasy map. The aesthetic ages well.
4. **Adding a new project takes one ho.** Thirty minutes, via CMS plus AI-assist. Not an afternoon of spreadsheet maintenance. The catalog is alive because keeping it alive is cheap.
5. **The site joins the small set of personal-work sites that do something structurally interesting** rather than just look good. Worrydream-adjacent. Matuschak-adjacent. Appleton-adjacent. Different from each. Worth doing.
6. **The practitioner can talk about the work in new ways** after building this. *Held as something to learn, not a hard success criterion.* The new vocabulary the site makes legible becomes the vocabulary used in conversation, outreach, interviews. This is a side effect the practitioner wants.

## What I Want to Learn

This is a communication tool, not a learning vehicle. The technical skills above will be acquired in passing. The real learning is structural and lives at the boundary of communication and form.

**Whether the interference cartography demonstrates coherence the way I want it to, or produces a different reading than I intend.** I expect what gets designed first will not be right. I am not anxious about that — it is the iteration loop being honest about itself. Learning here is the loop between design intent and design effect, run for as long as it takes for the form to do the work.

**What relationships, cracks, and adjacencies the cartography surfaces that I have not seen yet.** I know the work is one thing on a deep spiritual level. I have always known. The map will not validate the coherence to me. It will *discover* the texture of it — which works are unexpectedly close, which ridges I had not drawn, which towns sit between which peaks. That is the unexpected return on the build.

**How the public demonstration changes the way I talk about the work.** Interviews, outreach, applications, conversations with the curious. I want this to change. I do not yet know how. Figuring out how is part of the work the site is for.

What I am explicitly *not* trying to learn: whether having the site live affects how I make the next work. The site is a communication tool. A means, not an end. The next work will be made because it needs to be made, regardless of whether the previous work has been plotted onto the map. The map is downstream of the work, not upstream.

How to architect a static web frontend that orchestrates LLM API calls via a serverless Worker proxy — a transferable pattern for any future tool that needs in-browser AI capability without exposing API keys.

## Open Questions

What the seed leaves for the next document in the Kamae chain — and beyond.

**For System Design to resolve.**

- **Sveltia vs Decap CMS.** Both fit the architectural pattern. Choice depends on author-side UX and integration cost. To evaluate during System Design.
- **AI-assist runtime: local script or GitHub Action.** Local is faster iteration; Action is portable and lives where the data lives. Probably both, in different phases.
- **Work positions on the map.** Hand-placed (editorial), procedurally derived (algorithmic, from theme + relationship density), or hybrid (anchor works hand-placed, descendants arranged procedurally around them). Likely hybrid. Needs design work.
- **Map orientation per filter.** Fixed canvas with only elevations shifting, vs. reprojected per filter so each Venice has its own north. Calvino discipline pulls toward the latter; Stonecrow Hill discipline pulls toward the former. The seed does not pick; the visualization design will.
- **`work_groups` vocabulary.** Current candidates: Methodology, Production Systems, Constructive Interference Tools, Knowledge Architecture, Interactive Demonstrations, Adaptive Tools, Infrastructure (Kṣetra-Ops), Personal Utilities, Position & Manifesto, Writing, Photography, Archived. Review and finalize during data population.
- **Photography deployment.** Subdomain (`photography.sageframe.net`) vs. path (`sageframe.net/photography`). Signals whether photography is a distinct practice or a tighter integration with the catalog.
- **Interactive peak-dragging.** Whether to expose visitor-as-Marco-Polo. Physics supports it. UX call to be made.

**For the writing.**

- **The operational sentence.** "The practice of building tools, methodologies, and writing that hold the practitioner's presence inside AI-mediated work." Needs sharpening. Under-claims the pre-AI work; slightly over-claims the AI-dependency. The soul-sentence — *an epistemology of learning, creation, and craft* — is locked. The operational sentence is still in workshop.

**For mobile.**

- How "filtered Venice" navigation works on a narrow viewport with limited interaction. Stonecrow Hill renders beautifully on a desktop screen; on a phone, the contour density needs different handling. Likely a tighter cartography on mobile, or a strong default to the grid view. Decide during design and test.

**Permanently parked — decided not in scope.**

- **Resonance Field crosslink.** atmarcus.net keeps its hardcoded `HERO_PROJECTS`. The two systems stay separate. They are aesthetic siblings, not data siblings. The mathematical family is shared. The data sources are not.
